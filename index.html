Three moves, then the mailbox is real.

The console asks for one Google decision at a time, then shows the address it actually reached.

01

Connect

Create a fresh Google Cloud project, turn on the Gmail API, and download a Desktop OAuth client. The screen names the click that usually goes wrong, so a Web client never pretends to be ready.

02

Prove

FinMail calls your mailbox and shows two things: the address it connected as, and how many messages it can see. Subjects stay in Gmail. Sending stays locked.

03

Approve

New invoices arrive as a short WhatsApp list. A draft sends only when you reply with its number. “Yes” is not an approval. Dry run can still hold the message back.

The quiet rules.

FinMail is a local console for the person who owns the mailbox. It is not a shared inbox.

Scope

Read-only until you mean it.

The first grant is view-only. The send scope is a later step, after the connection test has already succeeded, and it needs a new token.

Secrets

Files, not a cloud vault.

credentials.json and token.json live on the computer that runs the agent. The console reads them from disk. It does not offer a place to paste them into someone else’s server.

Kill switch

Armed is a choice you can see.

SEND_ENABLED starts off. While it is off, an approval on WhatsApp is refused and the draft stays put. You can remove FinMail from the Google account that granted it.

myaccount.google.com/permissions

The moment setup is finished

Proof is an address, not a feeling.

When the test passes, the console prints the mailbox it reached and a count. That is the gate. Everything about sending comes after it.
